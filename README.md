# Frame stack, Jonze stretch, Sorang and Rooms

Two passthrough modes and two pieces played to a song, in one page.

**Frame stack** cuts a video into slices and stacks them along a time axis on a real table, with a filmstrip you scrub by touch.

**Jonze stretch** pinches the scanned room and pulls. The spot under your fingers follows your hand, the surface behind it stretches like taffy with its real texture, and a long pull smears into streaks of the column you grabbed. One hand or both. Let go and it springs back.

**Sorang** hangs a painting four metres tall in the dark and plays it slowly through its song. It takes on depth, splits into 100 slices by depth, turns out to be a mosaic of 100 stock photos, and then bursts on the drop into small glowing pieces that drift around you and fade with the song. On the desktop or in VR.

**Rooms** floats five clips in a grid frame, each as a frame stack: its frames as glowing layers along a time axis, with the clip itself travelling through them. A camera films them to a song, flying between the overview and the stacks through the verses and cutting on the beat through the drops. Desktop only.

## Run

```
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the HTTPS URL it prints in the Meta Quest browser (same network). Pick a mode, then press **Enter passthrough**.

### Frame stack

Rest an index fingertip on a table and hold still for about a second. The slider appears under your finger with the stack behind it.

- Hover just above the strip to preview frames. Touch and slide to scrub. Lift to stay on that frame.
- The round button on the left plays and pauses; the one on the right changes speed.
- **Move** puts it somewhere else. Your real hand occludes the table graphics.
- **Load video** on the 2D page slices your own clip (up to 128 frames).

### Jonze stretch

Finish **Space Setup** on the headset first, allow the camera when the page asks, then pinch the room and pull, with one hand or both.

- At rest nothing is drawn: you see plain passthrough. Only the surface you pull is drawn, and it feathers back into the real room at its edges.
- Pull sideways, up or down and the spot under your fingers slides along the surface. What's behind it stretches like taffy and keeps its real texture. Past about 11° of pull, as seen from your head, the part nearest your fingers starts smearing into streaks along the pull, and by about 35° they are long stripes in the surface's own colours. In angle, so a far table streaks after the same hand move as a near one.
- Pull toward yourself and the pinched spot comes with your fingers like cloth pinched between them. Pinch a surface right under your fingers (a table, the paper on it) and the spot follows your fingertips whichever way they go: drag it toward your chest and it leans after them as a pointed tent while its base drags along behind (only 40% of a pull toward you slides; sideways still slides in full), and lift your hand and it rises. Pinch a far wall and the tip sits on your line of sight through your fingers, just behind them, coming the same share of its way as your hand came of its own (up to 70%, and 1.2 m). In between it blends the two. Its sides run taut to a soft hem, five folds crease down them (shaded, so they read in a flat photo), and the hem is drawn in a little toward the pinch. Past the photo frozen for it, it shows the real room. The `pull …` line says what held it, `lift.30(hand)`, `(tip)` when it sits just behind your fingers, or `(cap)`, and how near the surface was, from `n1` (on it) to `n0` (across the room). A drag toward you streaks like a slide of the same size, and very long pulls also carry the pinched spot's colours up into the tip. Walls, and tables pulled straight up off themselves, also burst a little outward from your fingers.
- Hold an index fingertip within about 25 cm of a surface without pinching, and the surface is sucked up toward it: a narrow neck rising out of a wide base, drawn in hard toward the point under your finger. About 5 cm at 20 cm away, 10 cm at 15 cm, then reaching to just short of your fingertip up close. It follows your finger across the surface and springs back with a wobble when you move away. It rises from whatever is under the finger (a book on the table, not the table under it). It shows a recent clean photo of that spot, or after half a second without one, the live view with your hands cut out (your finger covers the cut). Pinch from there and it becomes a normal pull. Each one prints a `hover … from … to … photo bank|live|none` line.
- Push a wall in like a box. First map it: when you enter, the card says to map the wall. Pinch one corner of the area you want on a wall and drag to the opposite corner; a white frame follows your fingers along the wall, and letting go sets it (at least 30 cm each way). That area stays mapped for the session. Then raise an open palm on it, facing it in front of your eyes (ready for a high five), and hold it still for a moment: it gives a couple of centimetres. Push your palm forward and the area sinks into the wall as a box exactly that size: its back is the wall itself, its sides are the rim's colours drawn out along the depth into streaks, darker the deeper they go. Everything standing against the wall inside the frame, up to 1 m out (a fridge, the counters, a TV unit), goes in with it as part of its picture; anything further out, like a coffee table, stays in front. About 20 cm of push takes it 0.8 m in, the same look at any distance, up to 2.5 m. Lower or close your hand and it stays in. Raise a palm on it again to push it deeper, or pull your palm back toward you to bring it out (let go nearly flat and it pops back). Pinch inside it and it springs back out with a wobble, and the frame is back, ready to push again. A second palm on the box joins the push. While mapping, pinches draw the frame; after it, they stretch as usual. The box shows one clean photo with no hand anywhere on it, plus a second frame picked to cover what the first one missed; anything still past them reads the photo's nearest edge, so the far ends streak rather than show holes. The console prints `map R wall 2.4m 3.10x2.40` when you set the area, `push R wall …` when a palm takes hold, `push R rest .92m …` when you let go, and `push R? …` when an open palm won't (no map, off the map, not facing it).
- Swallow the room with a vacuum. Make a fist in front of you (index and middle curled into the palm, within about 45° of where you look) and aim it like a nozzle: a ring sits on the fist, and shards in a cone in front of it shake loose, then swirl the same way down a funnel into the fist. It starts as a trickle you can follow, a handful of pieces a second, and opens wider the longer you hold, until the whole room goes in. Each piece takes about two seconds, keeps its photo until it is close, then shrinks into the fist. Where one has ripped free is pitch black. Open your hand and the catching stops; pieces already in the air finish their trip, and what's gone stays black. Make a fist again and it carries on. When the last one lands there is a thunk, and everything past the scan goes black at once, leaving only your hands. It stays that way until you leave stretch mode, the session ends or you recentre. While it's up, pinches, fingertip spikes and the palm push stand down. The shards wear up to six photos picked from the last ones the camera took as you looked around (look around a little first for the fullest picture); where none covers a shard it smears the nearest photo's edge. The console prints `fist R .41 .38 .36 .44 tip .45 .38 L6.7` when a fist arms, `fist R? … why` when a curled hand won't (not a fist, cone, just let go of the box, room not ready), `void R start 480 shards`, `void catch 40/480 36/s cone 31°` while it sucks, and `void gone 12.4s worst 16.1ms p95 12.8ms` when the room is gone.
- Let go and that hand springs back with a wobble. Two hands work together. Pinch with both at once and each stretches the surface as it was, so pulling them apart stretches what is between them. Pinch with the second hand after the first has stretched something, and it grabs what you see.
- The pull plays notes. A pinch strums a chord, pulling climbs a pentatonic scale, a soft hum and a sparkle join as the streaks bloom, and letting go falls back and lands on a home chord. Each hand's notes come from the spot it grabbed.
- The picture comes from a small bank of camera frames taken while your head was steady, with your hands and forearms cut out of each one. A grab picks the best recent frame that covers the spot with no hand on it, plus a second frame behind it that fills whatever the first is missing. Where neither has the picture, you see the real room. If no frame covers the spot at all, the pinch lets go instead of bending something you can't see. Look around for a second after entering.
- Every scanned mesh, walls and furniture alike, is merged and subdivided to about 5 cm within 1.5 m of you, coarser further away, so anything bends smoothly. Tables, floors and walls that Quest detected as planes are flat: where the mesh lies within about 2 cm of a plane, facing the same way and inside its outline, it is snapped onto the plane. A pinch on a plane grabs the plane's point and normal, including through clutter up to 8 cm high on a table. The console's `pinch …` line says `hit=plane:table`, or `hit=mesh` with the nearest plane it rejected, and which scan was hit.
- Your real hands and arms stay in front of a stretch. Depth-only spheres on the joints and a capsule up each forearm, aimed at the elbow from a shoulder model, hide the stretch behind them. They trail tracking by 30 ms, the delay of passthrough's own image of the hand, except the pinching fingertips. Where the headset's depth map is available it also cuts the stretch wherever the real surface is at a tracked hand's depth along your line of sight, so the hand's real edge shows. A mug or a lamp beside your hand is not cut.
- Without a headset, `?preview=1` (or any browser without passthrough) runs the same material on a stand-in room. The wardrobe pulls apart on its own, and a webcam stands in for passthrough. `?preview=1&demo=push` loops a 4 m box pushed into the stand-in wall instead. `?preview=1&demo=void` loops a fist vacuuming the stand-in room (`&at=3.2` holds it at 3.2 s, for screenshots; `&voidcover=1` tints the shards red where a photo covers them and blue where they fall back to its edge).
- A small console panel at the upper left of the headset view shows the page's console: camera, photo and pull lines, warnings and errors. It is on under `npm run dev`; `?debug=0` hides it for recordings, `?debug=1` forces it on in a build.

### Sorang

Pick **Sorang** on the 2D page. The card folds down to a **Show card** button and the painting plays in the window. **Enter VR** plays it in the headset, where you stand in front of it.

- It opens as the clean painting, 4 m square, its lower edge just off the floor: on the desktop you look up at it from a person's eye height, and in VR you stand about 4.5 m in front of it. The song starts once the painting, its depth and the photos have landed and the browser allows sound: the tab click allows it, or else the first click or key (the page says so).
- It plays as one slow arc through the song (`public/sorang/music.mp3`, 2:40), always forward and never on the beat: the motion eases from one stage into the next. Over the opening swell (3–12 s) the bright paint pushes toward you and settles onto 100 sheets, one per band of depth. Through the quiet verse (14–31 s) a bright contour sweeps from the nearest sheet to the farthest, and behind it each tile turns out to be a stock photo, darkened or lightened to match the paint. On the build (31–45 s) the sheets fan toward you while the desktop view swings about 20° to the side.
- On the first drop (45 s) the tiles burst out from a point behind the centre, nearest sheets first, and over the next ten seconds settle into slow orbits around you with fine dust and a few faint lines. About 3% fly three times larger. They drift there while the song plays on, and fade to black with it as it dies away (136–152 s).
- The pieces glow: a bloom picks out what is bright and spreads it, gently on the painting and fully once the pieces fly. It works in the headset too, in both eyes, at a quarter of the eye's resolution, so the frame rate holds. `?bloom=0` turns it off for comparison.
- Everything scales with the painting's size: the relief, the fan, the burst, the orbits and the dust. The orbits centre on you and follow you slowly as you walk.
- On the desktop: click the view to look with the mouse, WASD walks, Shift is faster, and Esc releases the mouse. R runs the pieces home and starts the song again, Space pauses, M mutes. Once the song has faded out a click fades the painting back in and plays it again. Esc shows or hides the card once the mouse is free. Before the first look or step, moving the mouse shifts the view a little so the depth shows.
- In VR: a trigger or pinch plays it again once it has faded out, and A (or X) restarts it. A session started from the headset browser's own button, with no click on the page, gets its sound from the first trigger or pinch; if none comes within 8 s it plays in silence, and the music returns the next time it plays after one.
- **Load image** plays any JPEG, PNG or WebP instead, to the same song. Its depth comes from the in-browser model, which downloads the first time (about 25 MB), so it waits on the flat picture until depth arrives. If the model can't load, brightness stands in for depth.
- The default painting's depth is baked, so it never needs the model. Depth Anything reads a photo of a flat painting as a tilted card, so where a flat plane explains nearly all of the depth Sorang removes the tilt and lets brightness carry most of the relief: bright paint stands forward. Real photos keep the model's depth.
- With reduced motion the view doesn't swing or sway, the burst is smaller and the orbits are slow.
- If the music can't play (blocked, or it fails to load) the piece plays on a silent clock, in the same time. `?music=0` forces that, so `ecs_step` advances it exactly.
- `?mode=sorang` opens straight into it. `?sorangT=<song seconds>` freezes it at that moment of the song (no parallax or sway), and `?ui=0` hides the card, for repeatable screenshots: `?sorangT=20` is the photo scan, `40` the fan, `46` the burst, `70` the orbits and `145` the fade. `window.__sorang` has `seek`, `freeze`, `pause`, `play`, `rewind`, `setBloom`, `stage`, `phase` and `stats()` for tests. The headset console pins a `sorang bloom mode=…` line: `multiview` on Quest, `stereo` in the emulator, `mono` on the desktop.

Assets: `public/sorang/painting.jpg` is the painting, perspective-cropped from a screenshot by `scripts/sorang/crop_painting.py`. Its depth was baked with `node scripts/bake-depth/bake.mjs public/sorang/painting.jpg public/depth/sorang-painting --size 512` (stills skip ffmpeg). `public/sorang/photos.jpg` and `photos.json` are 100 photos from [Lorem Picsum](https://picsum.photos), by Unsplash photographers under the Unsplash License, built by `scripts/sorang/build_atlas.py` with fixed seeds. `photos.json` credits each one. `scripts/sorang/song_map.py public/sorang/music.mp3` prints a song's loudness by section and its strongest low-end onsets (where the drops are), for re-timing `SONG_MAP` in `src/sorang-song.ts` to a different song (needs ffmpeg and numpy).

### Rooms

Pick **Rooms** on the 2D page. The card folds down to a **Show card** button and the piece plays in the window. It's desktop only, so there is no Enter button.

- It opens on the empty frame seen from high above: a grid floor and two grid walls. The song (`public/rooms/music.mp3`, 2:42, 110 BPM) starts once the browser allows sound: the tab click allows it, or else the first click or key (the page says so).
- Each room is a frame stack, like Frame stack's: 48 frames of its clip, feathered at the edges, spaced along a time axis (the first frame at the front). The clip itself plays sharp and lit inside it. As it loops, its frame travels back through the stack, lighting a short trail of the frames around it. Each stack is the shape of its clip: the car park, the hallway and the rooftop are wide; the dark room and the train are nearer to square.
- Over the two-bar intro the camera cranes down while the five stacks build up one after another, top first, frame by frame.
- Verse (4–39 s): eased flights every two bars, each landing on the downbeat. The camera goes round a stack and then turns face on to see that memory in full. Face on, the clip plays at the front of its stack with the layers glowing out behind it. While a room is in view the others dim, and any stack in the way fades out. Every shot keeps moving while it holds: a front view pushes in, a 3/4 view turns round the stack, an oblique view glides down its time axis.
- First drop (39.3 s): a short glide to the wide view, landing on the beat. Then a glide every bar, through front, 3/4, oblique and top views, punching in on each, and a quicker glide every beat in the last bars.
- Break (74–109 s): flights again, with the overview turning slowly around the stacks. Second drop (109.1 s): glides every bar, then every two beats, then every beat. Outro (144 s): one long pull back and up while the stacks come apart frame by frame, one a bar, then black.
- Everything answers the beat grid: the trail and glow swell on each kick, a band of light climbs the walls each bar, a ring runs across the floor on each beat, strongest in the drops, and the bloom swells on the drops.
- Keys: Space pauses, R starts the song again, M mutes, ←/→ jump a bar (for checking cues). Moving the mouse leans the overview a little. Esc shows or hides the card. When the song ends it fades out and starts again from the top. A click does that too.
- **Clips:** each room plays `public/rooms/videos/NN.mp4` (01–05) on a loop, muted, as its sharp frame. Its stack's layers come from `NN-frames.jpg` beside it: 48 frames sampled evenly through the loop, eight across, cut back into layers when the page loads, so nothing is seeked out of the video in the browser. Each room's `aspect` in `src/rooms-layout.ts` matches its clip, so nothing is cropped. A room without its files shows a numbered test card that names the file it wants. `scripts/rooms/prep_clips.sh` makes both: it scales to 540 px high (cropping to 16:9, or keeping each clip's own shape with `--keep-aspect`, never upscaling), applies a phone's rotation flag, strips the sound, encodes H.264 with a keyframe every second, can crossfade the end into the start for a seamless loop (`--loop-fade 0.4`), and then bakes the frame sheet (`scripts/rooms/bake_frames.sh`). See `public/rooms/videos/README.md`.
- **The edit** lives in `SHOTS` in `src/rooms-score.ts`, one cue per line on the bar grid. For example, `cut(26, ql(3))` glides to room 3 from the left at 3/4, landing at bar 26, and `fly(16, front(4), 3)` lands face on to room 4 at bar 16 after a three-beat flight. A `cut` after the opening plays as a short glide (at most a beat and a half, and never more than two-thirds of the gap) with no flare. The other angles are `q`, `ob`/`obl` (low along the stack) and `top`. Changing one cue never shifts the others. The sections and energy sit beside it. Where the rooms stand, their sizes, shapes, stack lengths and yaw, and the overview presets (`wide`, `mid`, `high`, `low`, `side`, `orbit`, `rise`, `pull`) are in `src/rooms-layout.ts`.
- The song's grid was measured with `scripts/sorang/song_map.py`: beat 1 at 0.045 s, with sections every 16 bars. Re-time `SECTIONS` and `SHOTS` from its output to use another song.
- If the music can't play, the piece runs on a silent clock in the same time. `?music=0` forces that. `?mode=rooms` opens straight into it. `?roomsT=<song seconds>` freezes it at that moment, with every clip held on the frame it would show then. `?ui=0` hides the card, and `?bloom=0` turns the glow off. For example, `?roomsT=6` is the overview, `13.5` a front view, `23` an oblique view, `33` a 3/4 view, `39.4` the first drop, `61.5` a top view and `150` the outro. `window.__rooms` has `seek`, `freeze`, `pause`, `play`, `restart`, `setBloom`, `shot`, `section`, `bar` and `stats()` for tests, and the console prints a `[rooms] shot=… bar=…` line at every new shot.
- A lens sits on the picture. The stack in view stays sharp, layers included, and only what is clearly nearer or farther softens. A fine LED grid shows on the sharp parts and fades in the blur. `?lens=0` turns it off. With reduced motion the blur is half.
- With reduced motion the camera doesn't shake, leans half as far, and the roll and dolly are half.
- A face-on view draws all 48 layers of a stack across the whole screen. On a weak GPU, lower `LAYERS` (`src/rooms-assets.ts`) and re-bake the sheets with `bake_frames.sh --layers N`.

## Tuning

- `src/layout.ts`: stack size, strip length, touch and hover heights.
- The `FrameStack` component (scene JSON or the editor inspector): ghost, trail, length, lift, feather, glow.
- The `StretchLook` component: pull gain, sideways reach, stretch ramp (it grows with distance past 0.8 m), where streaks start (degrees of pull; 90 turns them off), photo feather, wobble, spring stiffness and damping, the burst and lift, and the pinch ripple.
- Matching the camera to passthrough: the lens model is measured for Quest 3 (863 px at 1280 wide, tilted 11.8° down, in front of its own eye) and the left room camera is used. Frames are posed 25 ms before their capture stamp. `StretchLook` has photo exposure and warmth, plus developer-only lens trims (focal, pitch/yaw/roll, offset, side) and camera latency; any non-neutral trim is printed to the console. For a quick headset check without a build, the URL can set the focal length, yaw, pitch and frame delay: `?lensf=863&yaw=-0.23&pitch=-11.77&tau=0.025`. The `lens …` line shows which came from the URL.
- Checking that alignment: `?debug=1&lens=overlay` draws the live camera in 48-px diagonal stripes over the room while nothing is pinched, each frame paired with the pose it was taken from, like a photo. Hold still and look along an edge: where it continues straight across the stripes, the camera model matches passthrough. A copy that is bigger everywhere points at focal length, bigger only up close at the forward offset, a constant shift at the mount angles or side, and an error only while turning at timing.
- Checking the hand cut: `?debug=1&occ=debug` shows the hand occluders 30% green, where the depth cut may act faint cyan, and what it cuts magenta instead of cutting it. `?debug=1&occ=delta` paints the real depth against the scanned room: white where they agree, red where the real surface is nearer, blue where it is farther, over 8 cm. The console keeps its `depth …`, `xr multiview …`, `camera pick …` and `lens …` lines on screen. `StretchLook` has the occluder lag as a developer trim.
- Timing a pull: each pull prints two `onset` lines at release, with its first 12 frames: frame times, how long the photo freeze and that frame's update took, how far the surface had moved (cm) and the fade (0-9). A hitch is one long frame; a pop is a jump in D.
- `MAX_LAYERS` in `src/frame-stack-system.ts`: raise it if the headset holds frame rate.
- The `RoomsLook` component (the "Rooms" node): ghost frames, trail, feather and glow (as in Frame stack), beat response, how far other rooms dim in a front view, cut flare, mouse parallax, bloom strength and threshold, and music volume. Where the frame stands is the node's position.
- The `SorangLook` component (the "Sorang painting" node): painting size, tiles across (each one photo), dust, relief depth and slice fan (both shares of the size), radial push, photo contrast, mouse parallax, bloom strength and threshold, and music volume. Where the painting hangs is the node's position. The stage windows are in `src/sorang-timeline.ts`, and which moment of the song plays which stage is `SONG_MAP` in `src/sorang-song.ts`.
